## Gamepad Control Scheme (Xbox and PlayStation)

The developer explicitly recommends playing with a dedicated controller. The combat rhythm is tailored around fluid thumbstick panning and shoulder-button strikes reminiscent of classic action RPGs. 

Getting comfortable with the **false hero controls** on a standard controller layout lets your fingers rest naturally on defensive and offensive triggers without sacrificing camera control. Both modern Xbox wireless controllers and PlayStation DualShock/DualSense controllers are natively supported.

### Controller Layout Comparison

| Action / Command | Xbox Controller Input | PlayStation Controller Input | Gameplay Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Movement** | Left Stick (LS) | Left Stick (L3) | Omnidirectional character locomotion |
| **Camera Control** | Right Stick (RS) | Right Stick (R3) | Rotate view and track targets |
| **Lock-On Target** | Press Right Stick (RS Click) | Press Right Stick (R3 Click) | Toggles target lock on hostile entities |
| **Light Attack / Combo Starter** | Right Bumper (RB) | R1 | Quick weapon slashes, early combo chains |
| **Heavy Attack / Finisher** | Right Trigger (RT) | R2 | Charged blows that stagger shielded foes |
| **Evade / Dash / Roll** | B Button | Circle Button | Grants invulnerability frames (i-frames) |
| **Jump / Traverse** | A Button | Cross (X) Button | Platforming and vaulting over low obstacles |
| **Interact / Loot** | X Button | Square Button | Open chests, speak to NPCs, pick up items |
| **Steal Combat Art (Mask Ability)** | Y Button | Triangle Button | Siphons slain enemy skills or executes stolen art |

When using a gamepad, your right index and middle fingers handle your main damage dealing, while your thumb stays anchored to the face buttons for dodging and executing mask abilities. Player experience indicates that keeping lock-on engaged during multi-target encounters prevents wild camera swings when dodging through larger enemy attacks.

## The Mask System and Combat Arts Input Flow

The defining mechanic of the game is your character's ability to harvest combat arts from defeated monsters and bosses. Fluency with **false hero controls** lets you steal these techniques and weave them seamlessly into standard sword combinations.

Unlike standard equipment-based soulslikes that require you to swap weapons in deep menu screens, your mask acts as an instant catalyst. When you defeat a foe possessing a unique art, pressing the interact or siphoning input locks that move into your active arts bar.

### Combat Inputs and Ability Trigger Mechanics

| Combat Stage | Controller Sequence | PC (KBM) Sequence | Resulting In-Game Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Attack Opener** | `RB` -> `RB` | `LMB` -> `LMB` | Delivers two standard light slashes to stagger |
| **2. Art Chaining** | `RB` -> `Y` | `LMB` -> `Q` | Instantly cancels the light attack into a stolen art |
| **3. Divine Modifier** | `LT` + `RT` | `F` + `RMB` | Consumes god power to unleash a heavy sacred strike |
| **4. Quick Disengage** | `B` (Tilt LS Back) | `Spacebar` + `S` | Executes an evasive backstep with invincibility frames |
| **5. Ability Harvesting** | `Hold X` near corpse | `Hold E` near corpse | Transfers the defeated creature’s move into your mask |

Because the combat system prioritizes skill mastery and chaining rather than grinding levels, inputs have strict buffering rules. Mashing buttons will queue unintended actions and leave you vulnerable to counterattacks. Instead, tap each command cleanly as the previous animation reaches its visual impact point.

## Advanced Movement, Evasion, and Combo Chaining

Mastering boss encounters demands an understanding of recovery frames and hit reaction timings. Refining your **false hero controls** during high-intensity fights means knowing when to roll through an attack versus when to use positional spacing.

Player testing from the community demo highlights that evasion carries distinct recovery windows depending on whether you perform a standing backstep, a directional roll, or a god-empowered dash.

### Evasive Maneuver Performance & Buffer Windows

| Movement Technique | Input Method | i-Frames Duration | Stamina / Flow Cost | Best Usage Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neutral Backstep** | Tap `B` / `Space` with neutral stick | Short (Approx. 0.2s) | Minimal | Dodging horizontal swipes while staying close |
| **Directional Roll** | Tap `B` / `Space` + Direction | Medium (Approx. 0.35s) | Moderate | Rolling through sweeping boss projectiles |
| **God Dash** | `LT` + `B` / `Shift` + `Space` | Long (Approx. 0.5s) | High (Consumes Power) | Escaping massive area-of-effect (AoE) slams |
| **Sprint Slide** | `Sprint` then tap `B` / `Space` | Minimal (Positional) | Low | Closing the distance on ranged spellcasters |

### Combo Execution Tips

1. **Avoid Over-Buffering:** The input buffer registers commands up to roughly half a second in advance. If you queue a heavy attack while an enemy is winding up a counter, you cannot cancel out of it.
2. **Weave Stolen Arts Between Light Swings:** Light attacks (`RB` / `LMB`) feature the shortest animation recovery. Use them as quick openers before deploying heavier stolen arts to prevent interruption.
3. **Use Lock-On Discretion:** When facing large mechanical beasts or arachnid horrors, disabling lock-on can give you better manual control over your directional rolls, allowing you to reposition behind their blind spots.

## Recommended Settings and Calibration

Configuring your **false hero controls** properly in the options menu ensures responsive gameplay and eliminates unwanted camera jerkiness during acrobatic boss sequences.

### Optimal Gameplay & Controller Settings Calibration

| Setting Option | Default Value | Recommended Value | Reasoning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Controller Deadzone** | 15% | 5% – 8% | Decreases stick delay for faster dodge reaction times |
| **Camera Sensitivity (X-Axis)** | 50% | 65% – 70% | Allows swift visual tracking when enemies dash behind you |
| **Camera Sensitivity (Y-Axis)** | 50% | 55% – 60% | Smooth vertical tracking for aerial and towering foes |
| **Auto-Target On Kill** | Off | On | Automatically shifts lock-on to the next nearest target |
| **Camera Shake / Bobbing** | 100% | 20% – 40% | Keeps the screen stable during heavy impact attacks |
| **Vibration Intensity** | 100% | 50% – 70% | Provides tactical hit feedback without distracting aim |

If you encounter stick drift on older controllers, raise your deadzone incrementally by 2% until character creep disappears. For PC players using high-DPI gaming mice, lowering the in-game mouse sensitivity prevents erratic camera turns during fast swipe attacks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the default False Hero controls on a controller?
On an Xbox gamepad, movement is handled by the Left Stick, camera by the Right Stick, Light Attacks by `RB`, Heavy Attacks by `RT`, Dodging by `B`, and Jumping by `A`. Your Mask Ability (used to steal and execute enemy arts) is mapped to `Y`, while `LB` handles blocks and `LT` triggers God Power stances. PlayStation controllers mirror this with `R1` for light attacks, `R2` for heavy attacks, `Circle` for dodging, and `Triangle` for mask abilities.

### Can I customize and remap the False Hero controls?
Yes. The in-game settings menu allows players to remap keyboard bindings freely. Controller users can either modify key bindings in the options menu or utilize Steam Input to build custom button maps, deadzone profiles, and custom paddle configurations for elite gamepads.

### Is False Hero better played with a controller or keyboard and mouse?
The solo developer behind False Hero explicitly recommends playing with a controller. The omnidirectional movement and fluid combination of bumper attacks and face-button evasions are optimized for gamepads. However, playing on keyboard and mouse is fully supported and works effectively if you rebind critical combat functions like the Mask Art to extra mouse buttons.

### How do I use stolen enemy attacks using the controls?
Once you defeat an enemy possessing a combat art, approach and hold the interact button (`X` on Xbox, `Square` on PlayStation, or `E` on PC) to harvest the skill into your mask. Once equipped, activate the move in combat by pressing the Mask Art button (`Y`, `Triangle`, or `Q`/`Mouse 4`), which can be chained directly after standard weapon attacks.
